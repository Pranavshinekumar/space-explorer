BLACK HOLES

Regions of space where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape.

What is a black hole?

A black hole forms when a huge amount of matter is squeezed into a very small space. Its gravity becomes so strong that the escape speed passes the speed of light. You can't see a black hole directly, but you can see the glowing gas around it and the way it bends the light of stars behind it.

Parts of a black hole

Every black hole has the same basic structure.

Event horizon

The point of no return. Anything that crosses it, including light, can't come back out.

Singularity

The center, where our current physics predicts matter is crushed to infinite density. Scientists still don't know what really happens there.

Accretion disk

A spinning ring of gas and dust that falls toward the black hole. Friction heats it until it glows brightly in X-rays.

Types of black holes

Black holes come in very different sizes.

Stellar

Born when a massive star runs out of fuel and collapses in a supernova.

About 3 to 100 times the Sun's mass

Intermediate

A rarer middle size. Astronomers have found only a few strong candidates.

About 100 to 100,000 solar masses

Supermassive

Sits at the center of most large galaxies. How they grew so big so fast is still an open question.

Millions to billions of solar masses
